What the check looks for

C2PA Content Credentials

A signed record inside the file saying it was made by AI, and by which tool. The standard the EU Code of Practice names.

Invisible watermark

An ID hidden in the pixels (Adobe TrustMark). It survives compression, resizing and social uploads that strip metadata.

IPTC digital source type

The metadata field Meta and Google read to show "AI info" labels on images.

It also spots traces that generators leave behind: Stable Diffusion WebUI, ComfyUI, InvokeAI, NovelAI, Midjourney and more.

Questions

Who has to mark AI images?

Under Article 50(2) of the EU AI Act, providers of generative AI systems whose output reaches people in the EU must mark it in a machine-readable way. That includes apps built on someone else's model. It applies to new systems since 2 August 2026, and to systems already on the market from 2 December 2026. The EU Code of Practice asks for at least two layers, such as signed metadata plus a watermark.

"No signals" means the image is human-made?

No. It means nothing in the file declares AI origin. Most platforms strip metadata on upload, and this checker reports evidence, it doesn't guess from pixels.

Is my image stored?

No. It's processed in memory on our server in the EU (Frankfurt) and discarded. Checks are limited to 10 per hour per visitor.

Is this legal advice?

No. MarkLayer is a technical tool. Whether and how you must mark content depends on your role and product; check with a lawyer.
